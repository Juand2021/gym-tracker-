# LLM accuracy for calorie/macro estimation from meal descriptions, and best practices for an LLM food logger

Scope: text descriptions in Spanish (Colombian foods), OpenAI/Azure OpenAI gpt-5-mini by default. Research done October 2026. Note: several numbers below come from search-result summaries or abstracts rather than full texts. These are flagged.

## 1. How accurate are LLMs at estimating energy and macronutrients (text, and briefly photos)?

### Takeaway
Frontier LLMs give useful but rough estimates. For single common foods, about 35–66% of calorie estimates fall within ±10% of database values. On whole meals, the best text-only result is carbs within ±7.5 g about two-thirds of the time (GPT-4o with chain-of-thought), with MAE of about 8.6 g. Photo-only calorie MAPE is about 30–36%, and it falls to about 14–18% when ingredients and amounts are given. Fat is usually the worst macro. Large portions are systematically underestimated. Non-US cuisines do worse.

### Cited Findings
**Text-based**
- **NutriBench** (UCSB, ICLR 2025; latest arXiv v6). The dataset has 11,857 meal descriptions from 11 countries (WWEIA + FAO/WHO GIFT), labelled with carbs, protein, fat and kcal. The meal descriptions were generated by GPT-4o-mini from intake records, and portions are given in grams or in natural units. The paper tested 12 LLMs. Only carbohydrates were evaluated, even though the other labels exist. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6); [ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ef3a57e4f26b640e6f90d78cbb011feb-Abstract-Conference.html)
  - Best model was GPT-4o with chain-of-thought (CoT): 66.82% of estimates within ±7.5 g, MAE 8.61 g, 99.16% answer rate. Averaged over the 12 models, accuracy was 43.2% with the base prompt and 47.5% with CoT. GPT-4o-mini with CoT beat GPT-4o with the base prompt. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6)
  - CoT helps most on multi-item meals, where base-prompt MAE grows with the number of items. MAE also grows with total carbohydrate content. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6)
  - Most models do worse with natural portions ("a cup of rice") than with metric ones. GPT models are the exception. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6)
  - Results varied by country. GPT-4o CoT had MAE of 2.20 g for Nigeria and 15.12 g for Sri Lanka. Countries with carb-heavy meals had higher error. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6)
  - On 72 US queries, nutritionists working without lookup scored 42.45%, while GPT-4o CoT scored 60.56%. One nutritionist allowed to look up foods reached 59.72%. The nutritionists took about 43 minutes in total, compared with about 2 minutes for GPT-4o-mini. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6)
  - Rewording the prompt made little difference: GPT-4o-mini scored 51.4% with the original wording and 51.5% averaged over 4 paraphrases. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6)
  - Only MAE was reported, so the paper does not show whether errors lean toward under- or overestimation. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6)
  - In an earlier version, GPT-3.5 with CoT was the best model at 51.48% accuracy. — [NutriBench v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v1)
- **Hoang et al., JAMA Netw Open, Dec 2023.** The study covered 222 food items in English and Traditional Chinese, compared GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 against Taiwan FDA food composition data, and collected data in Sep–Oct 2023. About 35–48% of energy estimates fell within ±10%. There was no significant difference from nutritionists for energy, carbs or fat. GPT-4 performed better but overestimated protein. *Source: secondary coverage, not the full text.* — [HealthDay](https://www.healthday.com/healthpro-news/health-technology/chatgpt-provides-accurate-nutritional-information); [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/01/30/ai-and-chatbots-can-provide-useful-nutritional-advice-but-can-t-replace-human-nutritionists/)
- **"AI Dietitian" (Nutrition, 2023/24), ChatGPT-3.5, 236 single foods compared with USDA.** The share of estimates within 10% ranged from 29.9% for fat to 66.4% for energy. 97% of energy estimates were within 40%. Complex recipes were not tested. *Source: search snippet; the full text was paywalled (403).* — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0899900723003532); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/376417116_AI_Dietitian_Unveiling_the_accuracy_of_ChatGPT's_nutritional_estimations)
- **Peruvian lunches (medRxiv preprint, Oct 2025; Carrillo-Larco et al.).** The study used 510 dishes, with Gemma-3 4B/12B/27B estimating from text descriptions. The best model had MAE of about 108 kcal and 26 g of carbs, with low agreement overall. The authors note that models fail on cuisines outside their training domain. This is the closest Latin-American evidence found. It did not test GPT models. — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.21.25338310)
- **Chinese Food Composition Table comparison (2026).** ChatGPT ranked 9th, with a mean ICC of 0.711 and a mean MAPE of 90.28%. Gemini, ChatGPT and Mistral were stable across repeated runs. Claude, Kimi and DeepSeek were less stable. *Source: search summary of a Frontiers in Nutrition 2026 paper.* — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1952757/full)
- **Text-only NHANES 24-h recalls (arXiv 2509.13268, 2025).** An open-source quantized LLM with a 10-shot CoT prompt had energy MAE of 652 kcal per day. After parameter-efficient fine-tuning, MAE dropped to 171–191 kcal and Lin's CCC rose from below 0.46 to above 0.89. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13268)
- **Ready-meal labels (Nutrients 2025).** Chatbots overestimated fat (122.6%) and carbs (136.3%) and underestimated sodium. None consistently matched dietitians. GPT-4o's calorie and macro estimates were consistent across runs (CV below 15%). — [PMC12526241](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12526241/)

**Photo-based (brief)**
- **ChatGPT-5 (Plus, Aug 2025), 195 dishes; Rodríguez-Jiménez et al., Nutrients 2025.** Energy MAPE was 30.5% with the image only (MAE 123 kcal), 24.4% when non-visual descriptors were added, 13.9% with the image plus an ingredient list with amounts (MAE 53 kcal), and 18.1% with the ingredient list only. Fat was the worst macro (MAPE 52.9% image-only, 24.2% with ingredients). With the ingredient list only, protein was overestimated because the model treated meat weights as cooked rather than raw. Bowls produced the largest errors. — [PMC12655113](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12655113/)
- **ChatGPT-4, 114 photos; O'Hara et al., Nutrients 2025.** The model underestimated weight for 76.3% of meals and was poor on medium and large portions. Median energy difference was only 0.1%. ICC with dietitians was 0.56 for energy, 0.67 for protein and 0.31 for carbs. Food identification precision was 93%. — [PMC11858203](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858203/)
- **GPT-4o vs Claude 3.5 Sonnet vs Gemini 1.5 Pro, 52 photos (Curr Dev Nutr, Sep 2025).** Energy MAPE was about 35.8% for GPT-4o and Claude. Gemini's errors across nutrients were 64–110%. Large portions were systematically underestimated. *Source: search summary.* — [PubMed 41081011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41081011/)
- **GPT-4o on simple vs complex meals.** Errors were substantial, especially fat in complex meals, and improved when context was added. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S088915752501659X)

**Apps compared**
- A vendor blog claims about ±16% error for Claude, ±18% for GPT-4o and ±22% for Gemini. It also cites a "BJN 2025" study saying tracking apps have 5–8% daily error versus 18–25% for chatbots. No methodology is given, the author sells a competing app, and I could not verify the study. **Do not rely on these numbers.** — [Nutrola blog](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/how-accurate-is-chatgpt-for-calorie-estimates)

### Inferences
- With gpt-5-mini and explicit grams (e.g., "250 g de cerdo"), expect roughly ±15–25% error per meal. For vague portions ("una porción de arroz") error is likely larger, dominated by portion size and the oil used in cooking, which is the main source of fat error.
- No study tests gpt-5-mini specifically on text. Note that GPT-4o-mini with CoT already beat GPT-4o without it on NutriBench. Reasoning or CoT matters more than model size.
- Colombian dishes such as bandeja paisa, ajiaco, arepas, sudado, patacón and fritos fall in the "non-US cuisine" category, where errors are higher.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study tests GPT-5, gpt-5-mini, Claude 4.x or Gemini 2.x on text-only meal logging with gram-weighed ground truth.
- NutriBench evaluated carbohydrates only, not kcal, protein or fat.
- No independent study measures MyFitnessPal accuracy in a way that allows a head-to-head comparison with LLMs.
- I found no data on Colombian foods or Spanish-language input.

## 2. Does grounding in a food composition database (RAG / tool calling) improve accuracy?

### Takeaway
Grounding is mixed. It clearly helps with **food identification and mapping** and gives an auditable per-item record. Retrieving nutrient values beats older models' guesses (DietAI24 with GPT-4V). For newer models it is only marginal for raw kcal accuracy, and NutriBench and Open-KNEAD (2026) find direct estimation by current models matches naive RAG. The best pattern is: the LLM decomposes the meal and estimates grams, retrieval finds candidate foods, the LLM picks the match, and code computes the nutrients.

### Cited Findings
- **DietAI24** (Commun Med 2025). This is GPT-4V / GPT-4 Turbo at temperature 0, with RAG over FNDDS (5,624 foods). The vision model describes the image, the model expands that into queries for vector search, the model picks food codes and a standard portion mapped to grams, and code computes nutrients from per-100 g values. On ASA24, energy MAE was 47.7 kcal versus 168 for the best commercial baseline. On Nutrition5k it was 68.2 versus 81.8 kcal for plain GPT Vision, a difference that was not significant (p≈0.13). The paper reports a 63% lower MAE overall. Limitations: international cuisines are missing, and mixed dishes and small portions are harder. — [PMC12589391](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12589391/)
- **NutriBench.** For GPT-4o-mini, RAG gave only minor gains and RAG+CoT was worse than CoT alone. For Llama 3.1, RAG helped on metric portions. On natural portions, CoT alone beat RAG+CoT, because the database stores per-100 g values and the model still has to convert portions. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6)
- **Open-KNEAD** (arXiv, Jul 2026). The authors report that retrieval grounding no longer beats current MLLMs' direct estimates. Their agentic approach (decompose into items, ground each item to an FNDDS code with nutrient-aware retrieval, apply a recipe prior to recover cooking-added energy) improves portion estimates and gives an auditable per-item record. On dietitian-verified ACETADA it beats two frontier closed models' direct portion estimates by about 30% and 53%. Without the recipe prior, invisible cooking energy (oil) biases estimates for non-US cuisine. *Source: abstract only.* — [arXiv 2607.12911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12911)
- **NutriRAG** (medRxiv 2025). On free-text diet logs, food classification Micro-F1 rose from 73.84 with GPT-4 to 82.24 with RAG. — [PMC11957177](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11957177/)
- **Mapping self-reported foods to databases** (J Nutr 2026). Zero-shot Claude Sonnet 4 reached 62.6% versus 48.9% for Haiku 3. The best approach was embedding similarity for the top-k candidates, then the LLM choosing one or "no match". Embedding retrieval reached 87.8% on ASA24→FooDB. Giving the LLM the whole database in context performed worse. *Source: search summary.* — [PubMed 42309308](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42309308/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022316626003275)
- **Fine-tuning** on FDC-derived data brought Gemma2-27B to MAE 9.57 g and 61.7%, close to GPT-4o CoT. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6)

### Inferences
- Recommended pipeline: (1) the LLM, with reasoning, returns structured items with Spanish name, normalized name, grams, cooking method and added fat. (2) Code looks each item up in a local table using embedding or fuzzy search plus LLM selection, and falls back to the LLM's own per-100 g estimate if nothing matches. (3) Code computes the totals.
- For Colombia, a local table matters more than USDA. The ICBF *Tabla de Composición de Alimentos Colombianos* is the obvious candidate (not verified here). Seed it with roughly 100–200 frequent foods (arroz blanco cocido, arepa, papa criolla, plátano maduro frito, fríjol, carne de res/cerdo cocida, huevo frito, aguacate, panela, etc.) stored per 100 g, specifying cooked vs raw.
- Keep the LLM's direct estimate as a cross-check. If the database result and the LLM result disagree by more than about 30%, flag the item for user review.

### Gaps
- I found no RAG study for Spanish or Latin American foods.
- The full Open-KNEAD results were not readable; only the abstract was.

## 3. Portion size error (humans and LLMs) and handling vague portions

### Takeaway
Portion size is the single biggest error source, for people and for models. People's self-reported errors range from -43% to +156% depending on the food. LLMs underestimate large portions. Vague terms ("una porción", "un plato") should resolve to explicit default grams shown to the user, with a clarifying question only when an item is a large share of the meal's calories.

### Cited Findings
- In a feeding study, adults' self-reported portions ranged from -43% for condiments to +156% for pasta. — [Penn State (Portion size estimation: a source of error)](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/portion-size-estimation-a-source-of-error-in-diet-assessment-stud/)
- When estimating portions from memory, only 15.6% of estimates were within about 10% of the true weight. A single average photo led to underestimation, while a series of 8 photos reduced error. Household measures were closer than photos. — [Cambridge BJN, food photography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/food-photography-i-the-perception-of-food-portion-size-from-photographs/E6915CA4E3897F27470DB2091B8AFED6); [search summary of portion literature](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/646660/portion-size-estimation-a-source-of-error-in-diet-)
- LLMs: ChatGPT-4 underestimated weight in 76% of meal photos and was worst on medium and large portions ([PMC11858203](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858203/)). Three LLMs systematically underestimated large portions ([PubMed 41081011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41081011/)).
- NutriBench: natural-language portions cause more error than gram amounts for most models. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6)
- DietAI24 casts portion estimation as choosing among standard database portion descriptors that map to grams. — [PMC12589391](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12589391/)
- Adding non-visual descriptors (fat type and amount, meat type) cut GPT-5 energy MAPE from 30.5% to 24.4%. Adding amounts cut it to 13.9%. — [PMC12655113](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12655113/)

### Inferences
- Use a default-portion table with explicit grams for each "porción". For example, "1 porción de arroz" could default to about 150 g cooked (a placeholder, not sourced). Return `portion_source: "user" | "default" | "estimated"` and show grams so the user can edit them.
- Return a per-item `confidence` (low/med/high) and a `clarifying_question`. Only ask when a vague item is large in kcal, such as oil, fried foods or rice in a carb-heavy plate. Ask at most one question per meal, and pre-fill it ("¿El cerdo era frito o asado?").
- Learn the user's own portions: after the user edits "porción de arroz" to 200 g, store that as their personal default.
- Treat LLM confidence scores as heuristic. I found no calibration evidence.

### Gaps
- I found no study on whether clarifying questions improve LLM food-log accuracy, and no evidence on how well LLM confidence scores are calibrated for nutrition.
- I found no sourced standard portion grams for Colombian dishes.

## 4. Underreporting in self-reported diet (doubly labelled water) and mitigation

### Takeaway
Compared with doubly labelled water, self-reported energy intake is systematically under-reported. One pooled estimate puts it at about 19% (preprint). App-based methods are not exempt. For a deficit or bulking app, logged calories should be treated as a biased signal and calibrated against weight trend, which is MacroFactor's approach.

### Cited Findings
- A systematic review of 59 adult studies found that most, including 16 technology-based methods, showed significant under-reporting against total energy expenditure, with few over-reporting. 24-h recalls showed less under-reporting. — [PMC6546220 (systematic review)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6546220)
- A pooled analysis of 11 DLW studies found intake under-estimated by about 19%. *Not peer-reviewed.* — [Preprints.org](https://preprints.org/manuscript/201710.0081/v1)
- Under-reporting is not explained by adiposity alone. Dietary restraint and socioeconomic status also contribute. — [Validity of self-reported EI by DLW (PubMed 11348556)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11348556/)
- A meta-analysis of 31 studies (n=4,518) examined sex differences by method. The pooled values were not extracted. — [PMC6928130](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6928130)
- MacroFactor positions Describe estimates as "close enough" for its expenditure and coaching calculations, which work back from weight trend. — [MacroFactor Help](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/216-log-foods-with-describe)

### Inferences
- Mitigations to build: reduce friction so the user logs every meal (chat or voice); add explicit prompts for commonly forgotten items (cooking oil, sauces, drinks, snacks, sugar in coffee); add an end-of-day "¿algo más?" check; and estimate TDEE adaptively from logged intake plus weight trend so systematic bias is partly absorbed.

### Gaps
- I did not extract figures for underreporting specifically with LLM or chat logging.

## 5. Engineering best practices

### Takeaway
Use strict JSON-schema Structured Outputs. Decompose the meal into items with grams and cooking method, listing oil, sauces and drinks separately. Compute totals in code, not in the LLM. Validate with Atwater checks and plausibility ranges. Always show an editable confirmation before saving.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI Structured Outputs (`text.format` with `type: "json_schema"`, `strict: true`) guarantees schema adherence. JSON mode only guarantees valid JSON. It is supported from GPT-4o onward. Handle `refusal` and incomplete responses (e.g., `max_output_tokens` reached). The first call with a new schema has extra latency. Use clear key names and descriptions, and include instructions for input that can't produce a valid response. — [OpenAI Structured Outputs guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs)
- CoT improves accuracy, especially on multi-item meals. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6)
- DietAI24 used temperature 0 and computed nutrients in code from per-100 g values times grams. — [PMC12589391](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12589391/)
- Cooking-added energy is invisible and must be modeled explicitly. Open-KNEAD's recipe prior addresses it ([arXiv 2607.12911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12911)). Fat is consistently the worst macro ([PMC12655113](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12655113/); [ScienceDirect GPT-4o](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S088915752501659X)).
- State raw vs cooked weight explicitly. GPT-5 overestimated protein by treating meat weights as cooked. — [PMC12655113](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12655113/)
- Model outputs vary between runs. ChatGPT was stable on the Chinese table, while Claude, Kimi and DeepSeek were less so ([Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1952757/full)). GPT-4o had CV below 15% on ready meals ([PMC12526241](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12526241/)).
- Prompt wording matters little, and the ±7.5 g accuracy is stable across paraphrases. — [NutriBench v6](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.12843v6)
- Matching to a database works better with embeddings for top-k candidates and the LLM choosing among them, or returning "no match". — [J Nutr 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022316626003275)

### Inferences (design recommendations; not directly sourced)
- **Schema per item:** `name_es`, `name_normalized_en`, `grams`, `state` (raw/cooked), `cooking_method`, `portion_source`, `confidence`, `kcal`, `protein_g`, `carbs_g`, `fat_g`, `alcohol_g`, `db_match_id|null`, `assumptions[]`. At meal level, add `clarifying_question|null`. Put a short `reasoning` field *before* the numbers so the model reasons first. Note that gpt-5-mini is a reasoning model, so reasoning effort may stand in for explicit CoT.
- **Temperature:** GPT-5-family reasoning models may not support custom temperature (verify on Azure). Use the default or the lowest allowed, and use `reasoning_effort` low or medium.
- **Decomposition rules in the prompt:** list cooking fat as its own item ("aceite de cocción", with a default by method, e.g. fried > sautéed > grilled); list sauces and condiments, drinks (jugos con azúcar, gaseosa, café con azúcar) and alcohol separately.
- **Validation in code:**
  - Check that kcal is within about ±10–15% of 4P + 4C + 9F + 7A. If not, recompute kcal from the macros.
  - Check kcal per 100 g against category ranges (e.g., cooked rice about 110–150, oil about 880–900).
  - Reject negative values and grams above about 2,000 per item.
  - Flag meals over about 2,500 kcal for confirmation.
  - The Atwater factors themselves are standard; the tolerance values are my own suggestion.
- **Confirmation UI:** show editable cards per item (grams slider, swap food), as MacroFactor Describe does. Save only after the user confirms.
- **Personalization:** store per-user portion overrides and frequent meals ("lo de siempre"), and inject them into the prompt as few-shot or defaults.
- **Language:** keep the Spanish name for display, have the LLM produce an English normalized name for USDA lookups, and prefer a Colombian table when one exists.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical study comparing schema designs, or the effect of JSON field order, on nutrition accuracy.
- I found no study testing whether translating to English before USDA lookup improves accuracy for Spanish input.
- I did not verify Azure-specific limits on temperature or structured outputs for gpt-5-mini.
- The OpenAI guide page I fetched recommended a model named "gpt-6-astra" for new projects. I did not verify this. It does not affect the gpt-5-mini choice, since Structured Outputs is supported from GPT-4o onward.

## 6. Commercial text/voice AI food logging examples

### Takeaway
Commercial apps converge on one pattern: parse free text or voice into generic database foods with quantities, then show an editable review before logging. I found no independent accuracy data for these features.

### Cited Findings
- **MacroFactor "Describe".** The user types or dictates through the keyboard microphone. It queries a database of common generic foods, not branded ones. It respects units (cups, grams) and accepts calorie-targeted descriptions ("500 calorie bean burrito"). The user taps "Find Foods", reviews and edits items and quantities, then logs. It struggles with compound descriptors, so the help article advises simple, generic descriptions and editing afterwards. — [MacroFactor Help](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/216-log-foods-with-describe)
- MacroFactor also offers photo and photo+text AI logging. A vendor page claims it integrated MyFitnessPal's database; this is unverified. — [FeastGood review](https://feastgood.com/macrofactor-review/); [MacroFactor vs Cal AI](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactor-vs-cal-ai/)
- The only accuracy figures for these apps come from competitor blogs, with undisclosed methods. Examples: MacroFactor "85% accuracy" and "±110 kcal/day" in Nutrola's tests. — [Nutrola](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/how-accurate-is-macrofactor)
- SnapCalorie and Foodvisor appear as baselines in DietAI24. DietAI24 beat both, e.g., carb MAE 6.95 g versus 21 g for SnapCalorie on ASA24. — [PMC12589391](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12589391/)

### Inferences
- Mapping to generic database foods plus an edit-before-log step is the industry pattern, and it is consistent with the research favoring grounding and auditability.

### Gaps
- I found no primary documentation on Cal AI's text logging, MyFitnessPal voice log, or SnapCalorie's text mode, and no independent accuracy evaluation of any commercial text or voice logger.
