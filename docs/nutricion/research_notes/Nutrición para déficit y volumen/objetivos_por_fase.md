# Evidence-based nutrition targets by phase (cut / bulk / maintenance-recomp) for resistance-trained people

Research date: 2026-10-24. Sources are peer-reviewed reviews, trials and official guidelines unless noted. Where a number came only from a secondary summary (news site, abstract snippet), that is flagged.

Worked example used in the Inferences sections: male, 25 y, 179 cm, sedentary job, lifts 4+ days/week, "higher body fat but not overweight". **Body weight was not given.** BMI < 25 at 179 cm means < ~80 kg, so the examples assume **78 kg and ~20–22% body fat (FFM ≈ 61–62 kg)**. Both numbers are assumptions; the app should use the user's real weight.

---

## 1. Cut: rate of loss, deficit size, lean-mass risk, aggressive short cuts, calorie floors

### Takeaway
Default to losing **0.5–1.0% of body weight per week** (Helms 2014), toward the low end when the person is leaner or wants to keep the most muscle. Bigger deficits cost more lean mass, but the size of the effect is uncertain: Murphy & Koehler suggest about 500 kcal/day as a cutoff, and a later reanalysis disputes it. A short, aggressive 3–4 week cut can work for someone with higher body fat if protein is high (~2.4 g/kg) and they keep lifting hard (Longland 2016).

### Cited Findings
- Helms, Aragon & Fitschen (JISSN 2014, 11:20): "caloric intake should be set at a level that results in bodyweight losses of approximately 0.5 to 1%/wk to maximize muscle retention." — [Helms 2014, PMC4033492](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033492/)
- Garthe et al. 2011 (elite athletes, n=24) randomized subjects to lose 0.7%/wk (slow) or 1.4%/wk (fast) of body weight, both with strength training. The slow group gained more lean mass and more upper-body 1RM during the intervention. The authors recommend ~0.7%/wk for athletes who want to keep or gain LBM and strength while losing weight. — [Garthe thesis/papers, NIH Brage](https://nih.brage.unit.no/nih-xmlui/handle/11250/171332)
- Garthe long-term follow-up (2011): both groups lost ~6% of body mass but were back to baseline after 12 months. Conclusion: rate of loss "is not the most important factor" for keeping the result long term. — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4023821)
- Murphy & Koehler, *Scand J Med Sci Sports* (online Oct 2021; Vol 32 Issue 1, 2022), meta-analysis of RCTs of RT with an energy deficit lasting ≥3 weeks. LBM gains were impaired with a deficit versus energy balance (ES −0.57, p=0.02). Strength gains did not differ significantly (ES −0.31, p=0.28). Meta-regression: a deficit of ~500 kcal/day prevented LM gains. Their advice is to avoid deficits >500 kcal/day if the goal is preserving LBM. — [TUM portal](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/energy-deficiency-impairs-resistance-training-gains-in-lean-mass-/); [MySportScience summary](https://www.mysportscience.com/post/can-i-gain-muscle-and-lose-weight-at-the-same-time)
- Critique (non-peer-reviewed blog reanalysis, 2025): the slope is shallow and not clearly significant, so the 500 kcal threshold is not robust. — [mdickens](https://buttondown.com/mdickens/archive/can-you-maintain-lean-mass-in-a-calorie-deficit/)
- Ruiz-Castellano et al. 2021 (*Nutrients*, narrative review on fat-loss phases in resistance-trained athletes): target 0.5–1.0% BW/wk, using the low end. In case studies more FFM was kept at 0.5%/wk than at 0.7–1%/wk; in one 1%/wk case, FFM loss was 42.8% of total weight lost. "The lower the body fat, the more conservative the deficit." — [PMC8471721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8471721/)
- Roberts, Helms, Trexler & Fitschen 2020 (*J Hum Kinet*, physique athletes): during competition prep, slower loss (≤0.5% body mass/wk) is preferable to limit FFM loss. — [Roberts 2020 (Duke record)](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1687463)
- Ruiz-Castellano 2021 also notes that the 3,500 kcal/lb rule overestimates loss. Hall's dynamic rule is about 55 kcal/day per lb of eventual loss: a 500 kcal/day deficit gives ~4 kg over a year, plateauing at about 12 months. — [PMC8471721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8471721/)
- **Aggressive short cut evidence:** Longland et al. 2016 (*AJCN*): young men, 40% energy deficit for 4 weeks, RT + HIIT 6 d/wk. The 2.4 g/kg/day protein group *gained* ~1.2 kg LBM versus 0.1 kg at 1.2 g/kg, and lost more fat (−4.8 vs −3.5 kg). — [PubMed 26817506](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26817506/) (effect sizes taken from secondary summaries: [MedicalResearch](https://medicalresearch.com/?p=21100))
- **Calorie floors (energy availability, EA = intake − exercise energy, per kg FFM):** for men, EA < 25 kcal/kg FFM/day is associated with more FFM loss and hormonal changes. For women, about 45 kcal/kg FFM is "optimal" and < 30 kcal/kg FFM brings hormonal disruption. — [Ruiz-Castellano 2021, PMC8471721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8471721/)
- Aggressive restriction is linked to greater FFM loss, especially at low body fat. — [PMC8471721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8471721/)
- Helms 2014 warns about eating and body-image disorders in physique sports. — [PMC4033492](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033492/)

### Inferences
- **App defaults for a cut:** target rate 0.5–1.0% BW/wk, default **0.7%/wk**, which matches Garthe and the middle of Helms. Offer "conservative 0.5%" and "aggressive 1.0%". Allow more than 1.0%/wk only for short phases (≤4 weeks) and only when estimated body fat is higher (e.g., men > ~20%). Show a warning that lean-mass risk rises.
- For the example user (assumed 78 kg): 0.7%/wk ≈ 0.55 kg/wk, about a 600 kcal/day deficit using the ~7,700 kcal/kg rule of thumb. 1.0%/wk ≈ 0.78 kg/wk ≈ 850 kcal/day. Over 3 weeks that is roughly 1.6–2.3 kg of scale weight. Early weeks will show extra loss from glycogen and water, which is not fat.
- **3-week deadline:** the evidence (Longland 2016, 4 weeks at −40%) says a short aggressive cut of ~25–40% deficit, or ~1%+ BW/wk, is acceptable for a person with higher body fat when protein is 2.2–2.4+ g/kg and training stays heavy. Strength is less sensitive to deficits than lean mass (Murphy & Koehler). The app should frame this as a time-limited "sprint" and follow it with maintenance (see section 7). It should not present this as the default rate.
- **Calorie floor:** derive the floor from EA rather than a fixed number. Men: intake − exercise kcal ≥ 25 kcal/kg FFM. Example: FFM 61 kg × 25 ≈ 1,525 kcal plus exercise kcal. A practical hard floor for men is about **1,500–1,600 kcal/day** before training energy is added. The 1,500 kcal figure is a common clinical heuristic, not sourced here; see Gaps.
- Estimating maintenance for the example user: a sedentary job plus 4+ lifting sessions roughly corresponds to an activity factor of ~1.5–1.6 × BMR. Mifflin-St Jeor BMR (78 kg, 179 cm, 25 y, male) ≈ 10×78 + 6.25×179 − 5×25 + 5 ≈ 1,779 kcal, so TDEE ≈ 2,650–2,850 kcal. Formula and arithmetic are mine; the activity factor is a heuristic. The app should re-estimate TDEE from the 2–3-week trend of logged intake against weight trend.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source found for a fixed "minimum kcal" (e.g., 1,500 men / 1,200 women). These are widely used clinical heuristics; the source was not verified in this session.
- Garthe 2011 exact effect sizes not retrieved (full text not accessed).
- How much the dose-response from Murphy & Koehler applies at higher body fat (more fat to lose → more tolerance for bigger deficits) is not directly quantified there. The Forbes/Hall body-fat-dependent partitioning theory was not fetched.

## 2. Protein (daily target, per meal, cut vs other phases, higher body-fat individuals)

### Takeaway
**1.6 g/kg/day** is the point estimate where extra protein stopped adding lean mass, with an upper CI of ~2.2 g/kg (Morton 2018). Use **1.6–2.2 g/kg** for maintenance and bulk, and **~2.0–2.4 g/kg BW**, or **2.3–3.1 g/kg FFM**, in a cut. Split across **3–6 meals of 0.4–0.55 g/kg**. For someone with higher body fat, base the target on FFM or goal weight, not total weight.

### Cited Findings
- Morton et al. 2018 (*BJSM*; 49 RCTs, n=1,863): protein supplementation added ~0.30 kg FFM during RT. Intakes beyond **1.62 g/kg/day** gave no further FFM gains. The benefit was larger in resistance-trained people and smaller with age. — [Stronger By Science summary](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=61316); [AUT repository](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/8dea98a4-4e37-4c60-be43-4b6ece0bd9a5)
- The Morton breakpoint's 95% CI spanned **1.03–2.20 g/kg** and the breakpoint itself was not statistically significant (p=0.079), so 2.2 g/kg is the "safe upper" default. — [Stronger By Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/?p=61316)
- ISSN Position Stand: Protein and Exercise (Jäger et al., JISSN 2017): 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day for most exercising people. Higher intakes (2.3–3.1 g/kg/day) may be needed to maximize FFM retention in resistance-trained people on hypocaloric diets. — [NutraIngredients summary](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2017/06/21/ISSN-publishes-position-stand-on-protein-and-exercise/); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/fcc34534a75d466aa37abda0042df7a7) (number taken from the secondary summary; the full text was not opened)
- Helms 2014: in contest prep, 2.3–3.1 g/kg **of lean body mass**/day. Have 3–6 meals, with 0.4–0.5 g/kg BW protein before and after training. Nutrient timing and frequency changes have "little effect" on fat loss or lean-mass retention. — [PMC4033492](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033492/)
- Ruiz-Castellano 2021 (fat-loss phase review): 2.2–3.0 g/kg BW/day. Cites Hector & Phillips (1.6–2.4 g/kg BW, higher for bigger deficits and leaner athletes), ISSN (no added benefit above ~2.6 g/kg BW), and Bandegan (~2.2 g/kg on rest days). Per meal 0.40–0.55 g/kg BW, ≥2.5 g leucine per feeding, 4–5 protein feedings. — [PMC8471721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8471721/)
- Roberts et al. 2020: 1.8–2.7 g/kg for physique athletes, with optimal intake "largely related to skeletal muscle mass." — [Roberts 2020 (Duke)](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1687463)
- Iraki 2019 (off-season/bulk): 1.6–2.2 g/kg/day, at least 1.6, with ~2.2 "more consistently optimal". 0.40–0.55 g/kg per meal across 3–6 meals, plus an optional pre-sleep protein meal 1–2 h before bed. — [Iraki 2019, PMC6680710](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6680710/)
- Barakat 2020 (recomposition): secondary summaries say the review converges on ~1.6–2.4 g/kg/day; the full text was not verified. — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4066252); [MacroFactor summary](https://macrofactor.com/recomposition/)
- General population: DGA 2025–2030 (released Jan 7, 2026) set a protein goal of **1.2–1.6 g/kg/day**, prioritized at every meal. — [AAFP summary, July 2026](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2026/0700/practice-guidelines-dietary-guidelines)
- Longland 2016: 2.4 g/kg in a 40% deficit beat 1.2 g/kg for both LBM and fat loss. — [PubMed 26817506](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26817506/)

### Inferences
- **App defaults (g/kg of body weight, for normal-weight users):** maintenance/recomp 1.8 g/kg (range 1.6–2.2); bulk 1.8 g/kg (1.6–2.2); cut 2.2 g/kg (2.0–2.6); aggressive/short cut 2.4 g/kg.
- **Higher body fat:** the sources state this in FFM terms (Helms 2.3–3.1 g/kg FFM; Roberts "related to skeletal muscle mass"). So, when body-fat% is known, compute the target from FFM. Otherwise use goal or "adjusted" body weight. Example (FFM ≈ 61 kg): 2.3–3.1 × 61 ≈ **140–190 g**. In BW terms, 2.2 × 78 ≈ 172 g. A default of **~165–175 g/day** fits both.
- Per meal: 0.4–0.55 g/kg × 78 ≈ 31–43 g, so **~35–45 g × 4 meals** for the example user.
- The tracker should treat protein as the **priority macro**: show it as the second daily ring/bar after kcal, and count it as "met" at ≥ 90% of target.

### Gaps
- No trial directly tests whether to use total BW, goal BW, or FFM in people with higher body fat. The recommendation above is inferred from the FFM-based wording of the reviews.
- ISSN 2017 exact wording was not read in full text.

## 3. Fat and carbohydrate ranges for lifters

### Takeaway
Fat: **20–35% of kcal** in maintenance/bulk (ACSM-based). In a cut, **15/20–30%**, never below **~0.5 g/kg BW**. Carbohydrate is "the rest", with sensible minimums of **~2–3 g/kg** in a cut and **3–5+ g/kg** in a bulk.

### Cited Findings
- Helms 2014 (contest prep): fat 15–30% of kcal, remainder from carbohydrate. — [PMC4033492](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033492/)
- Roberts 2020: keep fat at the low end, 10–25% of kcal, so more of the energy goes to carbohydrate for training. — [Roberts 2020 (Duke)](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1687463)
- Ruiz-Castellano 2021 (fat-loss phase): fat 20–30% of energy, and at least 0.5 g/kg BW/day if calories are very limited (Table 1: 0.5–1.0 g/kg). Diets ≤ 20% fat may lower testosterone (confounded). Carbohydrate 2–5 g/kg BW/day. Slater & Phillips' 4–7 g/kg is "uncertain"; 1–3 g/kg may not impair performance. A strength session depletes 24–40% of muscle glycogen. Ketogenic diets (<10% energy or ≤50 g/day) may be suboptimal for FFM retention but do not clearly reduce strength. — [PMC8471721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8471721/)
- Iraki 2019 (bulk): fat 0.5–1.5 g/kg/day and 20–35% of kcal, citing ACSM. Carbs are the remaining kcal, at least 3–5 g/kg/day. Male bodybuilders in the off-season average ~5.3 g/kg; strength-sport ranges cited are 4–7 g/kg and 5–6 g/kg. — [PMC6680710](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6680710/)
- ACSM/AND/Dietitians of Canada 2016 joint position (Thomas, Erdman & Burke; J Acad Nutr Diet 116(3):501–528; MSSE 48(3):543–568): nutrition strategies should be individualized by type, amount and timing. Bibliographic details confirmed. — [PubMed 26917108](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26917108/)
- WHO 2023: a wide carbohydrate range (40–70% of energy) is compatible with a healthy diet, coming mainly from whole grains, vegetables, fruit and legumes. — [SciMex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/new-international-dietary-guidelines-released) (secondary)
- DGA 2025–2030: saturated fat ≤ 10% of kcal. — [AAFP](https://aafp.org/afp/2026/0700/practice-guidelines-dietary-guidelines)

### Inferences
- **App algorithm:** protein first, then fat, then carbs as the remainder.
  - Fat default: cut 25% of kcal, floor max(0.6 g/kg, 20%) for most users, absolute floor 0.5 g/kg. Bulk/maintenance 25–30%.
  - Carbs: remainder. Warn if the result is below 2 g/kg in a cut or below 3 g/kg in a bulk.
- Example cut at ~2,100 kcal (78 kg): protein 170 g (680 kcal), fat 25% ≈ 58 g (≈ 0.75 g/kg; 525 kcal), carbs ≈ 895 kcal ≈ 224 g (≈ 2.9 g/kg). All values fall inside the cited ranges. Arithmetic is mine.

### Gaps
- The ACSM 2016 numeric tables could not be verified this session. The PDF was not text-extractable and abstracts do not carry the numbers. From memory and unverified: CHO 3–5 g/kg for moderate activity up to 8–12 g/kg for very high volume; fat 20–35% of energy and not < 20%; protein 1.2–2.0 g/kg, ~0.3 g/kg per meal. Verify against doi:10.1016/j.jand.2015.12.006 before citing in-app.
- ISSN 2017 "diets and body composition" position stand (Aragon et al.) was not retrieved.

## 4. Fiber and water

### Takeaway
Fiber: **14 g per 1,000 kcal** (US reference; ~38 g/day for men ≤50, ~25 g for women), with WHO's **≥ 25 g/day** from food as the floor. Water: **total** water (food + drinks) of ~**2.5 L/day for men and 2.0 L for women** (EFSA), or 3.7/2.7 L (US NASEM/IOM). Add extra for training and heat.

### Cited Findings
- WHO carbohydrate guideline (July 2023): adults should eat ≥ 400 g fruits and vegetables and **≥ 25 g naturally occurring dietary fibre** per day from foods (strong recommendation). — [SciMex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/new-international-dietary-guidelines-released); [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/medicine/who-dietary-guidelines-12102.html)
- 14 g fiber per 1,000 kcal, about 38 g/day for men and 25 g/day for women. The 38/30 g AIs for men 19–50/51+ come from the Institute of Medicine. Secondary sources disagree on whether DGA or IOM is the origin; it is IOM's AI, which the DGA adopts. — [ASN / nutrition.org](https://nutrition.org/most-americans-are-not-getting-enough-fiber-in-our-diets/); [Outgrow calculator (secondary)](https://outgrow.co/blog/fiber-intake-calculator-guide-2025/)
- US adults average ~8.7–9.9 g fiber/1,000 kcal; only ~7% meet the target. — [ASN](https://nutrition.org/most-americans-are-not-getting-enough-fiber-in-our-diets/)
- Ruiz-Castellano 2021 (fat-loss review) cites EFSA fiber of 25 g/day. — [PMC8471721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8471721/)
- EFSA 2010: adequate **total** water intake is 2.5 L/day (men) and 2.0 L/day (women) for people over 14 y. It assumes 70–80% comes from fluids and 20–30% from food, and does **not** account for extra losses from physical activity. US IOM: 3.7 L (men) and 2.7 L (women). — [PMC5133077](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5133077); [Redalyc (Mexican study)](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/3092/309246896006.pdf)
- DGA 2025–2030 summaries mention "sufficient dietary fiber" and water as the primary beverage, but no gram figure was found. Secondary sources conflict on how strongly fiber is emphasized. — [OSU Extension](https://extension.okstate.edu/fact-sheets/the-new-dietary-guidelines-for-americans-2025-2030); [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/01/08/protein-gut-health-and-opportunity-new-dietary-guidelines-promise-to-maha)
- Colombia GABA (ICBF/FAO, 2nd ed. Oct 2020, population > 2 y): 9 messages built around the "Plato saludable de la Familia Colombiana" (6 food groups), plus recommendations to drink water and be physically active. National implementation plan runs 2023–2026. — [Documento técnico (minambiente PDF)](https://www.minambiente.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/guias_alimentarias_basadas_en_alimentos_para_la_poblacion_colombiana_mayor_de_2_anos_0.pdf); [ICBF plan 2023–2026](https://www.icbf.gov.co/system/files/plan_nacional_de_implementacion_de_guias_alimentarias_2023-2026_dir_nutricion_8_marzo_2024.pdf); [UMariana column](https://revistas.umariana.edu.co/index.php/BoletinInformativoCEI/article/download/4478/4578/16125)

### Inferences
- **Fiber default:** max(25 g, 14 g × kcal/1,000). At 2,100 kcal that is ~30 g; at 3,000 kcal, 42 g. Show it as a "soft" target, met at ≥ 80%. Fiber also helps satiety in a cut (general knowledge; not sourced here).
- **Water default:** men 2.5 L total ≈ ~2.0 L from drinks; women ~1.6 L from drinks. Add ~0.5–1.0 L per training hour (heuristic, not sourced here). Logging water should be optional or low-friction. Water is the least evidence-critical metric.

### Gaps
- Exact Colombian GABA messages on fiber and water (e.g., glasses per day) were not extracted. The document needs to be read directly.
- No sport-specific fluid figure verified (ACSM fluid guidance not retrieved).

## 5. Diet breaks and refeeds

### Takeaway
Diet breaks (1–2 weeks at maintenance) do not reliably add fat loss in trained people, but they improve hunger and mood and may help adherence in long cuts (≥ 8–12 weeks). They are **not useful for a 3-week cut**. Refeeds have weak evidence and only short-lived hormonal effects.

### Cited Findings
- MATADOR (Byrne et al., *Int J Obes* 2018; online 2017; 51 men with obesity): 16 weeks of continuous restriction versus 8 × 2-week restriction blocks alternated with 2-week energy-balance blocks (30 weeks total). Intermittent restriction gave more weight and fat loss without more lean loss, and a smaller drop in RMR. Press reports ~8 kg more weight loss maintained at 6 months (secondary figure). — [PMC5803575](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5803575); [MedicalResearch](https://medicalresearch.com/?p=37058)
- ICECAP (Peos et al., *MSSE* 2021; 53(8):1685–1698; 61 resistance-trained adults, 32 women, mean BF 25.5%): 12 weeks of continuous moderate restriction versus 4 × 3-week restriction blocks with 3 × 1-week maintenance breaks. Per Ruiz-Castellano 2021, both produced similar fat loss and FFM retention. — [PubMed 33587549](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33587549/); [PMC8471721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8471721/)
- ICECAP secondary analysis (Peos, *PLoS ONE* 2021, n=26): a 1-week diet break produced +0.6 kg BW, +0.7 kg FFM (likely glycogen/water) and higher REE, with no fat-mass change. Leg muscle endurance improved but strength did not. Hunger and irritability were lower; fullness, satisfaction and alertness higher. Suggested for later in a cut, when appetite threatens adherence. — [PMC7906362](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7906362/)
- Ruiz-Castellano 2021: suggests diet breaks of 1–2 weeks at maintenance; ideal length and frequency are unknown and 2 weeks may be too long. Refeeds in bodybuilding are often 24 h, once or twice weekly. Dirlewanger: 3 days of carbohydrate overfeeding at +40% raised leptin 28% and energy expenditure 7%, and both returned to baseline once the deficit resumed. — [PMC8471721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8471721/)
- Roberts 2020: diet breaks and refeeds "may be beneficial". — [Duke](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1687463)
- A 2025 systematic review (Poon et al., *Nutrition Reviews*) on intermittent dieting with breaks exists but was not retrieved. — [mentioned in search results; no link verified]

### Inferences
- App: offer an optional "diet break" week (kcal = maintenance, keep protein) after every **6–12 weeks** of cutting, or when the user reports high hunger or fatigue. Default off for cuts of < 6 weeks, so off for the 3-week example. Tell users that the scale rises ~0.5–1 kg after a break or refeed from glycogen and water, not fat (consistent with Peos +0.6 kg BW).

### Gaps
- ICECAP primary results were read from a secondary review, not the abstract or full text.
- Poon 2025 meta-analysis findings not retrieved.

## 6. Body recomposition in trained people with higher body fat

### Takeaway
Recomposition (fat loss and muscle gain at roughly stable weight) is documented in trained people, not just novices. It is most likely with higher body fat, high protein (~1.6–2.4 g/kg), progressive RT, and roughly maintenance or a small deficit. That fits the example user.

### Cited Findings
- Barakat et al. 2020, "Body Recomposition: Can Trained Individuals Build Muscle and Lose Fat at the Same Time?" (*Strength Cond J* 42(5):7–21): "a substantial amount of literature" shows recomposition in resistance-trained individuals. The key factors are progressive RT plus evidence-based nutrition. — [LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4066252)
- Secondary summaries add: protein 1.6–2.4 g/kg; sleep and total stress matter; novices and people with higher body fat are the most likely to recomp. — [MacroFactor](https://macrofactor.com/recomposition/); [Iron Culture ep. 85](https://ironculture.libsyn.com/website/ep-85-body-recomposition-can-you-build-muscle-and-lose-fat-at-the-same-time)
- Longland 2016 (trained or overweight young men, −40% kcal, 2.4 g/kg): +1.2 kg LBM, −4.8 kg fat in 4 weeks, a recomp even in a large deficit. — [PubMed 26817506](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26817506/)
- Murphy & Koehler: strength gains were not significantly impaired by a deficit, even though lean-mass gains were. — [TUM](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/energy-deficiency-impairs-resistance-training-gains-in-lean-mass-/)

### Inferences
- "Recomp" mode in the app: kcal at maintenance to −10% (≈ 0 to −0.25% BW/wk) and protein ~2.0 g/kg. Track waist and strength trends rather than scale weight, because scale weight barely moves.
- For the example user after the 3-week event: a recomp or maintenance phase is a sensible next step.

### Gaps
- Barakat 2020 full text was not accessed, so exact protein and kcal recommendations come from secondary sources.

## 7. Bulking (surplus, rate of gain, lean vs aggressive, ending a bulk) and maintenance phases

### Takeaway
Use a **small surplus**. Novice and intermediate lifters: **+10–20% kcal, ~0.25–0.5% BW/week** (~1–2%/month). Advanced lifters: **+5–10%, ~0.25%/week** or less. Larger surpluses mainly add fat (Garthe 2013; Helms 2023). A bulk ends when fat gain becomes excessive, followed by a maintenance or cut phase.

### Cited Findings
- Iraki, Fitschen, Espinar & Helms 2019 (*Sports*, off-season bodybuilders): novice/intermediate +10–20% above maintenance at ~0.25–0.5% BW/wk; advanced +5–10% (Table 1) at ~0.25% BW/wk. ~2 kg/month is "too excessive" for advanced lifters. A faster gain may be acceptable right after a contest to restore health. Weigh daily or several times per week, use weekly averages, and adjust from the rate of gain and body-composition changes. — [PMC6680710](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6680710/); [PubMed 31247944](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31247944/)
- Garthe et al. 2013 (cited in Iraki): an ad-libitum group (~2,964 kcal) versus a group eating +~600 kcal more. Lean mass gains of 1.7 vs 1.2 kg were not significantly different, but fat gain was much higher (1.1 vs 0.2 kg). The authors suggest +200–300 kcal/day for trained athletes rather than +500. — [PMC6680710](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6680710/)
- Helms et al. 2023 (*Sports Med Open*; RCT, 8 weeks, maintenance vs +5% vs +15% surplus, n=17 completers, trained): no evidence of differences in muscle thickness or squat 1RM. The +15% group gained more bench 1RM and more skinfold thickness. Body-mass change strongly predicted skinfold (fat) change. Conclusion: faster gain mainly adds fat. — [PubMed 37914977](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37914977/); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/ab6ce09af2cc4daa9a1c2e9b88e1b1e9)
- Excess fat gain in a bulk lengthens or worsens the next cut. — [Iraki 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6680710/)
- Murphy & Koehler advise avoiding prolonged deficits when the goal is building lean mass. — [MySportScience](https://www.mysportscience.com/post/can-i-gain-muscle-and-lose-weight-at-the-same-time)

### Inferences
- **App defaults for a bulk:** a "training age" selector sets the rate. Novice: 0.5%/wk (~2%/month). Intermediate: 0.25–0.5%/wk. Advanced: ≤ 0.25%/wk (~1%/month). The kcal surplus is derived from the rate and then auto-adjusted from the weekly trend: +~5–15% kcal, roughly +200–400 kcal for most men.
- **Ending a bulk:** none of the retrieved sources give a fixed rule. Practical app triggers (inference): waist up more than ~X cm, or estimated BF above a user-set ceiling (e.g., men ~18–20%), or a planned end date. Then transition to 2–4+ weeks at maintenance before cutting.
- **Maintenance phases:** use them between a cut and a bulk, after an aggressive sprint (like the 3-week event cut), and as diet breaks. Raise kcal stepwise to the newly estimated TDEE and expect +0.5–1.5 kg of scale weight from glycogen and water (consistent with Peos +0.6 kg after a 1-week break). Ruiz-Castellano reports post-diet metabolic adaptation, lower NEAT and higher appetite, which supports returning to maintenance gradually and deliberately. — [PMC8471721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8471721/)

### Gaps
- "Ribeiro 2019" on lean versus aggressive bulking was not located. The closest controlled evidence is Garthe 2013 and Helms 2023. No source found specifying bulk duration or end criteria.
- No peer-reviewed guidance found on the ideal length of maintenance phases between cut and bulk.

## 8. What a good tracker should show (daily, weekly, adherence, realistic precision)

### Takeaway
Show **kcal and protein daily**: protein is the top-priority macro, then kcal; fat, carbs and fiber come second. Judge progress on **7-day averages** of weight and intake, not single days (Iraki 2019). Adjust targets from the 2–3-week trend. Treat ±5–10% on kcal as "on target".

### Cited Findings
- Weigh daily or several times per week and use weekly averages to dampen daily fluctuations. Adjust intake based on rate of change and body composition. — [Iraki 2019, PMC6680710](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6680710/)
- Weight change is the controlling variable: body-mass change strongly predicted fat (skinfold) change in a bulk. — [Helms 2023, PubMed 37914977](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37914977/)
- Rate targets are expressed as %BW per week in the major reviews, so the app should display rate as %BW/wk. — [Helms 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033492/); [Iraki 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6680710/)
- Static energy rules (3,500 kcal/lb) overestimate loss, and adaptation lowers expenditure over time. — [PMC8471721](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8471721/)
- Strength is preserved better than lean mass in a deficit, so strength trends (e.g., e1RM) are a useful in-app "muscle-retention" proxy during cuts. — [Murphy & Koehler](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/energy-deficiency-impairs-resistance-training-gains-in-lean-mass-/)
- Nutrient timing and frequency have little effect on outcomes, so meal timing need not be tracked as a KPI. — [Helms 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033492/)

### Inferences
- **Daily view:**
  - Primary: kcal remaining, as a ring or bar, with a tolerance band of ±10%.
  - Priority: protein grams against target, "met" at ≥ 90%.
  - Secondary: fat (show the floor warning) and carbs (no failure state; they are the remainder).
  - Tertiary: fiber and water.
  - Also: protein per meal (optional) and today's weight entry.
- **Weekly view:**
  - 7-day average weight and its change in %BW/wk against target.
  - Average kcal and protein.
  - Days protein was met (x/7) and days kcal was within band (x/7).
  - Waist (weekly) and strength trend from the app's existing workout data.
  - Estimated TDEE from intake versus weight trend, updated weekly after ≥ 2 weeks of data.
- **Adherence score (suggested):** a weekly composite, e.g., 40% protein days met + 40% kcal days in band + 20% logging completeness. A simple design inference; no validated score was found.
- **Precision:** display kcal rounded to 10–50 kcal and macros to 1–5 g. Make plan adjustments only when the 2–3-week trend deviates more than ~0.25% BW/wk from target. Food-label and logging error makes finer precision illusory (general knowledge; no specific citation retrieved).
- **For a 3-week deadline:** show a countdown and projected weight on event day from the current 7-day trend. Flag that early loss includes water and glycogen. Warn if the rate exceeds ~1.25% BW/wk or intake falls below the EA-based floor.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed validation of specific adherence-scoring formulas or tracking-UI designs was found.
- No retrieved source quantifies food-logging error in lifters. The commonly cited ~10–20% label/logging error was not verified this session.
